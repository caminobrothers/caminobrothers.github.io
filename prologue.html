The Camino Brothers Book

Buen Camino, Brother

A WhatsApp Story

by Michael Redmond, with Nicki Kuhle

Prologue

In late September 2019, I arrived in Porto to walk the Portuguese Camino to Santiago de Compostela. It was my fourth camino, and I set out on my own, the way I had before. But setting out alone had never meant walking alone. My first camino took me along the first five hundred kilometres of the French Way, as far as León, and it filled up with friends from many countries. That fall I went back and walked the rest of it, starting again at León: the first days with friends from the spring, new ones the rest of the way. I finished as a guest in the home of Russell and Eileen, friends I met and walked with on both trips, on Spain's east coast. They kept me for days and treated me like royalty. My third trip I did not set out alone at all. My daughters Sarah and Katie walked with me: two weeks of the French Way, from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Burgos, then a week in Portugal as tourists instead of pilgrims. They came home with the same love of Europe I had caught myself.

I met, walked with, and became friends with dozens of people over the first three caminos. Friends who, if I saw them again in 20 years, it would be hugs and reminiscing. Those friends aren't named in this book, but you know who you are. You were an important part of my journey.

So I knew how the Camino worked. I set out alone and people show up. I expected the Portuguese Way to do the same: new friends for two weeks, goodbyes at Santiago, and then back home. What I did not expect was that the new friends would become lifelong friends, walking camino after camino, year after year.

On the second evening, Monday, September 30, 2019, I stopped at Macedo's Bar for supper and a few drinks. It was a small village. At the next table sat two German brothers hiking the Camino for the first time. I had seen them hiking earlier in the afternoon, and myself and a couple of people I walked with for a few hours joked that they looked like they might make it to Santiago that day - they were full of business. I grabbed my bottles of wine and joined them at their table. A couple of hours of talking and laughing, and a little bit of English/German talk that made it that much better. By the end of the meal, and more wine, we had agreed to walk together the next morning.

The three of us walked together for the rest of that camino: about ten more days up the coast of Portugal, across the border bridge into Spain, and into Santiago de Compostela together, through the old town to the cathedral, on the same October day in 2019. It was an extraordinary camino friendship, which means it was a good one. At the end we said goodbye the way pilgrims do: see you down the road. We all knew we had to continue this.

Since that October day in 2019, we have walked seven more long trails together: the French Way again in Spain, the Rheinsteig above the Rhine in Germany, the Portuguese Way a second time, a week on Madeira, the Jakobsweg through Bavaria and on to Innsbruck, the Camino del Norte and the Francés in 2025, and the Tyrol in 2026. Eight caminos in all, about 1,877 kilometres of trail. When we are walking together, this chat goes quiet - we are side by side, saying it out loud instead. It fills the months between the walks: the planning, the day to day life, and everything else that happens in one's life over years and years.

This book is our WhatsApp chat: my messages and Nicki's, our own words, edited for length, with our photos, from October 2019 to October 2026. Nothing in it was written for an audience. That is the point of it, and most of its charm. This is not just a hiking story, this is also a story of friendship. Buen Camino, Brother.
