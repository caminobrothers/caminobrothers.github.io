How It Started

On Monday, September 30, 2019, Mike Redmond of Prince Edward Island, Canada sat down to supper at Macedo's Bar in São Pedro de Rates, a small village on the Portuguese Way. Nicki Kuhle of Hessen, Germany was at the table, with his brother Dennis. Mike and Nicki had never met before that night.

They walked on to Santiago together. The chat between Mike and Nicki started on October 2, 2019, and it never stopped - not between caminos, and not since. This book is that chat.

About the Book

Buen Camino, Brother is not a guidebook. It is a real WhatsApp chat, printed the way it happened.

In September 2019, Mike Redmond of Prince Edward Island, Canada met Nicki Kuhle, of Hessen, Germany, and his brother Dennis over supper in São Pedro de Rates, a small village on the Portuguese Way. They walked to Santiago together, and then they just kept going. Eight caminos in seven years: the Portuguese Way twice, the French Way, the Rheinsteig, Madeira, the Jakobsweg, the Norte and the Francés, and the Tyrol. About 1,877 kilometres of trail and 82 days of walking. Dennis walked with them on most of them.

Between the walks, the chat never stopped. It was always just the two of us in it, me and Nicki - Dennis walked with us on the caminos, but he was never part of the chat. This book is that chat - thousands of messages and the photos that went with them, from October 2019 to October 2026. The planning, the prices, the weather, the jokes, the hard days, and everything else that happens in two men's lives over seven years.

Nothing in it was written for an audience. It is a hiking story, and it is also a story of friendship.

If you have walked a Camino, are planning one, or just like a true story told the way it happened, this book is for you.

From the Prologue

In late September 2019, I arrived in Porto to walk the Portuguese Camino to Santiago de Compostela. It was my fourth camino, and I set out on my own, the way I had before. But setting out alone had never meant walking alone. My first camino took me along the first five hundred kilometres of the French Way, as far as León, and it filled up with friends from many countries. That fall I went back and walked the rest of it, starting again at León: the first days with friends from the spring, new ones the rest of the way. I finished as a guest in the home of Russell and Eileen, friends I met and walked with on both trips, on Spain's east coast. They kept me for days and treated me like royalty. My third trip I did not set out alone at all. My daughters Sarah and Katie walked with me: two weeks of the French Way, from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Burgos, then a week in Portugal as tourists instead of pilgrims. They came home with the same love of Europe I had caught myself.

I met, walked with, and became friends with dozens of people over the first three caminos. Friends who, if I saw them again in 20 years, it would be hugs and reminiscing. Those friends aren't named in this book, but you know who you are. You were an important part of my journey.

So I knew how the Camino worked. I set out alone and people show up. I expected the Portuguese Way to do the same: new friends for two weeks, goodbyes at Santiago, and then back home. What I did not expect was that the new friends would become lifelong friends, walking camino after camino, year after year.
